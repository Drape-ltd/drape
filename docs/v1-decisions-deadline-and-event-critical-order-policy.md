# V1 Decisions: Deadline And Event-Critical Order Policy

Date: April 2, 2026

## Why This Exists

For custom garments, deadline is part of the product value.

This document locks the working V1 stance on:

- how deadlines should be treated
- what sellers can promise
- what happens when event timing is at risk

## Decision 1: Deadline Is Part Of Scope

### Chosen rule

For `CUSTOM` orders, the customer deadline is part of the commercial scope.

### Why

- timing affects value, feasibility, shipping risk, and customer expectations
- event-based garments lose value sharply when the date is missed

## Decision 2: Quoted Completion Date Must Be A Real Commitment

### Chosen rule

The quoted completion date should be treated as a real seller commitment, not a decorative estimate.

### Why

- otherwise deadline capture is misleading
- customers need a trustworthy basis for accepting the quote

## Decision 3: Do Not Promise Rush Rescue As A Normal V1 Behavior

### Chosen rule

Drape should not imply that late custom orders can normally be rescued by rush handling.

### Why

- serious custom businesses already advise customers to plan well ahead
- rush rescue creates false hope and trust problems

## Decision 4: Consultation Should Be Encouraged When Timing Feels Risky

### Chosen rule

If the deadline is tight relative to the garment complexity or sourcing plan, consultation should be strongly encouraged before the quote is sent.

### Why

- it reduces timeline overpromising
- it helps align on material, fit, and feasibility early

## Decision 5: Deadline Does Not Silently Change After Acceptance

### Chosen rule

After acceptance:

- the customer cannot silently make the deadline earlier
- the tailor cannot silently move the deadline later

### Why

- deadline is part of the agreed scope

## Decision 6: Event-Date Misses Are High-Severity Support Cases

### Chosen rule

If the seller causes the garment to miss the event date, the issue should be treated as high severity.

### Why

- the customer may have lost most of the value of the order even if the garment still arrives later

## Decision 7: Seller Must Surface Delay Risk Early

### Chosen rule

If the tailor sees that the quoted date is at risk, they should surface that risk early rather than letting the order drift silently.

### Why

- silence makes deadline failures much worse
- early escalation gives support and the customer more room to react

## Recommendation Summary

The cleanest V1 stance is:

- deadline is part of scope
- quoted completion date is a real promise
- no casual rush-rescue messaging
- encourage consultation on risky timelines
- treat seller-caused event misses as high-severity support/remedy cases

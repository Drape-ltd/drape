Executive Overview

Drape is a two-sided mobile marketplace connecting customers with independent tailors and boutiques for custom and ready-made clothing. Built Africa-first with global reach, Drape digitises the entire custom-clothing journey — from discovery and measurement capture through quote acceptance, production tracking, and secure payout — removing the opacity and trust friction that plague offline tailoring relationships today.

"Find a tailor you trust, place one clear order, and follow it through."

Problem

For Customers

  • No reliable way to discover and vet skilled tailors
  • Orders placed over WhatsApp with no paper trail
  • No visibility into production progress
  • Disputes resolved informally, often in the tailor's favour
  • Measurements lost between orders; repeat work starts from scratch
  • Payments handed over with no escrow or protection

For Tailors

  • Client acquisition limited to word-of-mouth and social DMs
  • No professional storefront or portfolio hosting
  • Unclear briefs lead to rework and chargebacks
  • No tools to manage multiple concurrent orders
  • International and diaspora clients hard to reach and service
  • Earnings unreliable; no structured payout system

Solution

Drape provides a structured end-to-end workflow on top of which both parties can operate with confidence. The platform combines a verified tailor directory, guided measurement capture, a collaborative quoting system, escrow-backed payments, production-stage tracking with photo updates, and a post-delivery review system — all in a single mobile app available on iOS and Android.

The African fashion market is the fastest-growing apparel segment globally, yet the custom and bespoke clothing sector remains almost entirely offline. Drape's initial focus covers Nigeria, Ghana, and Kenya — three of Africa's largest consumer markets — while immediately addressing the diaspora customer base in the UK, EU, and US, who regularly commission tailors back home with no reliable platform to coordinate across time zones.

The global custom apparel market is projected to exceed $150 billion by 2030, driven by a consumer shift away from fast fashion toward individuality, quality, and cultural expression. Drape is positioned directly in this white space.

Business Model

Drape earns revenue on every completed transaction. The model is designed to align platform incentives with order success — Drape only gets paid when a customer receives their garment and the tailor receives their payout.

Payment & Payout Flow

Customers pay via Stripe (USD, GBP, EUR) or Paystack (NGN, GHS, KES). Funds are held in escrow and released to the tailor 72 hours after confirmed delivery — providing a dispute window without requiring active ops intervention on most orders. Tailor payouts are processed via Stripe Connect or Paystack's transfer API in the tailor's local settlement currency.

All FX conversions are shown as informational only; the source currency is the binding price. This removes ambiguity for both parties and eliminates silent re-pricing on exchange rate moves.

Drape's moat is not a single feature — it is the trust stack: verified profiles, production photo evidence, escrow payments, structured disputes, and a review system that protects both sides. This combination does not exist on any platform serving this market today.

Go-to-Market Strategy

Supply Side (Tailors)

Tailors are onboarded through a curated application funnel — portfolio review, ID verification, and payout account setup — ensuring quality before any profile goes live. This supply-gate strategy protects brand trust at launch and creates scarcity-driven demand from customers. Initial tailor acquisition targets Lagos, Accra, and Nairobi, with UK-based African tailors as a high-value second cohort.

Demand Side (Customers)

A public waitlist is live, segmented into customer and tailor funnels. Customer acquisition focuses on the African diaspora in the UK and EU — a high-intent, underserved cohort that already tries to commission tailors informally. Secondary demand comes from in-market customers in Nigeria, Ghana, and Kenya through social and community channels.

Operations & Risk

Operational Infrastructure

  • 73 documented ops policies covering payments, disputes, privacy, abuse, customs, and moderation
  • Ops control surface for disputes, bans, and moderation
  • Sentry (error monitoring) + PostHog (analytics) integrated
  • Automated quote expiry, checkout timeout, and payout release
  • App Store + Play Store submission pipeline ready

Key Risks & Mitigations

  • Supply quality → Curated onboarding, portfolio review, ID gating
  • Fulfilment disputes → Photo evidence at each production stage; structured remedy ladder
  • International shipping → Customs policy research complete; DDP/DDU disclosure required pre-checkout
  • Payment fraud → Stripe + Paystack fraud tooling; escrow model limits exposure
  • Platform abuse → Contact bypass detection, review retaliation prevention, abuse logging

Technology Stack (Summary)

Drape is built on a modern, cloud-native monorepo. The mobile app (React Native / Expo) targets both iOS and Android from a single codebase. The web presence (Next.js 15 / Cloudflare) handles the public site and waitlist. The backend is Supabase (PostgreSQL + Auth + Storage + Edge Functions) with Row-Level Security enforced throughout. Payments are handled server-side to prevent client-side tampering, with native PaymentSheet integration for mobile UX. The stack is low-cost to operate at early scale and has a clear path to horizontal scaling as transaction volume grows.

Why Now

  • Mobile penetration is at an inflection point across Nigeria, Ghana, and Kenya — the conditions for a marketplace to scale have arrived.
  • Fast fashion backlash is driving global consumer demand for bespoke, meaningful clothing — particularly among the African diaspora who want cultural garments made with integrity.
  • Payment infrastructure has matured — Paystack's API and Stripe's global reach make multi-currency, cross-border escrow viable for the first time at this price point.
  • The market is uncontested — no platform has successfully productised the African tailor-customer relationship at scale. The window to become the category default is open.

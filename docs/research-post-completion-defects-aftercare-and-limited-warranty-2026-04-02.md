# Research Notes: Post-Completion Defects, Aftercare, And Limited Warranty

Date: April 2, 2026

## Why This Exists

An order can look “done” and still go wrong.

What is still ambiguous for Drape is:

- what counts as a valid post-completion issue
- how long a customer should have to raise it
- when the answer should be repair, alteration, remake, partial refund, or no remedy
- how `READY_MADE` should differ from `CUSTOM`
- what happens if payout has already released

This note is the research layer for that ambiguity.

## High-Signal Takeaways

- Custom-fashion businesses usually use short, explicit fit and quality claim windows.
- They usually prefer alteration, remake, or repair before refund.
- They also usually distinguish fit or construction defects from ordinary wear and tear.
- Good aftercare systems rely on prompt, structured evidence like photos, measurements, and receipts.
- In infrastructure-variable markets, local repair and manual evidence intake matter more than polished reverse-logistics flows.

## 1. What Drape Already Decided

Drape already has strong direction on:

- remedy ladder instead of blanket refunds
- local alteration credit as a valid remedy
- reverse logistics being selective, not universal
- payout blocking while a concern or dispute is open
- `READY_MADE` behaving more retail-like than `CUSTOM`

What is still missing is a dedicated rule for:

- issues discovered after a customer receives or collects the garment
- latent workmanship defects after completion
- whether Drape offers any limited warranty-like support at all

Important Drape takeaway:

- the core remedy logic exists
- the post-completion support window is still too fuzzy

## 2. Current Marketplace Signal

## A. Proper Cloth treats aftercare as fit support, remake support, and bounded abuse controls

Proper Cloth’s current return and remake guidance shows several useful patterns:

- first custom orders can qualify for returns or remakes within `60 days`
- customers are encouraged to wash or wear garments normally enough to evaluate fit before deciding
- local tailoring reimbursement is an accepted remedy for minor tailored-clothing issues
- remakes generally require the original garment back
- extra remakes may be approved for manufacturing defects or garments outside accepted tolerances
- repeat abuse can move a customer onto a more restrictive policy

Important Drape takeaway:

- aftercare can be generous without becoming open-ended
- local tailoring and remake are both legitimate parts of the support model
- platforms still need abuse limits

## B. INDOCHINO uses short claim windows, fit-first support, and bounded local reimbursements

INDOCHINO’s current fit and quality guidance is more conservative:

- fit or construction concerns must usually be raised within `14 days`
- refunds are not offered for made-to-measure garments
- local alterations can be reimbursed up to defined caps
- if a garment is not alterable, the customer is asked for fit photos and a short description so the team can assess next steps

Important Drape takeaway:

- custom work can stay customer-supportive without promising retail-style refunds
- short deadlines and structured evidence reduce ambiguity

## C. Suitsupply reinforces the difference between ready-made and custom-like support

Suitsupply’s current support surface is useful because it is closer to standard retail:

- unworn, unaltered products can be returned or exchanged within `30 days`
- alterations are a normal in-store service
- altered-size support can still route to customer service and returns help in some cases

Important Drape takeaway:

- `READY_MADE` should keep a more retail-like logic than `CUSTOM`
- post-completion treatment should differ by order type, not just by defect severity

## D. Etsy’s case model reinforces structured evidence and prompt escalation

Etsy’s current order-help and case process is useful even though it is broader than apparel:

- buyers can escalate when an item arrives damaged or significantly not as described
- the process relies on case-log evidence
- evidence can include photos, measurements, and third-party evaluations

Important Drape takeaway:

- aftercare claims should not be one vague text box
- structured evidence matters even more once the order is technically complete

## 3. The Most Useful Drape Distinction

The strongest practical distinction is not:

- before completion
- after completion

The strongest distinction is:

- immediate fit or finish issue
- latent workmanship defect
- normal wear, misuse, or body-change issue

## A. Immediate fit or finish issue

Examples:

- wrong fit on first try
- seam issue noticed on first inspection
- obvious finishing issue
- damage present at delivery or collection

These should usually be:

- reported quickly
- handled inside ordinary remedy logic
- eligible for alteration, remake, repair, or stronger remedy depending on fault

## B. Latent workmanship defect

Examples:

- seam failure after very limited normal use
- button or zipper failure that points to poor construction
- construction issue that was not reasonably visible at first inspection

These should usually be:

- reviewable for a limited extra window
- more likely to support repair or remake than blanket refund

## C. Normal wear, misuse, care damage, or later body change

Examples:

- gradual wear after repeated use
- shrinkage from improper laundering
- staining, tearing, or damage from use
- body changes after the order
- unauthorized alterations that caused new problems

These should usually fall outside Drape remedy coverage.

## 4. African-Market Reality Strengthens The Case For Practical Aftercare

Drape’s operating context matters here.

A realistic aftercare model should not assume:

- easy same-city store visits
- smooth reverse shipping
- instant video support
- rich desktop tooling

Important Drape inference:

- local alteration, local handoff, and async evidence submission are not fallback hacks
- they are part of the real operating model

That means Drape should design aftercare to work with:

- mobile photos
- receipt uploads
- simple measurements
- support-assisted intake
- local repair where shipping would be slower or riskier than the fix itself

## 5. What This Means For V1

The strongest V1 direction is:

- use a short issue-reporting window for post-delivery fit and finish concerns
- allow a narrower extra window for credible latent workmanship defects
- distinguish `READY_MADE` and `CUSTOM`
- prefer repair, alteration, or remake before refund for custom work
- do not imply a lifetime warranty
- require structured evidence
- narrow support sharply once the issue looks more like wear and tear than initial workmanship
